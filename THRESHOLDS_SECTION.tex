\section{Threshold Selection and Sensitivity}
\label{sec:thresholds}

Four numeric parameters govern the framework's blocking behaviour. They are set out together here, with the evidence for each, because a defense whose thresholds are unjustified is not reproducible and its reported rates cannot be interpreted.

\subsection{L3 --- the anomaly threshold}
\label{subsec:l3_threshold}

\texttt{ANOMALY\_THRESHOLD} is the scale anchor for L3's blocking decision. L3 blocks when the anomaly score exceeds twice the effective threshold, where the effective threshold is reduced to $0.7\times$ its nominal value for queries L0 has classified HIGH. The operative bar is therefore $30.0$ for LOW and MEDIUM queries and $21.0$ for HIGH ones.

Because L0--L3 inspect the query text only --- no retrieval, no model inference --- the entire decision is reproducible from the generators alone. A full sweep is consequently cheap enough to run for every candidate value rather than argued from a single point. Table~\ref{tab:l3_sweep} reports that sweep over five seeds, evaluating attacks and \emph{both} benign sets at each candidate so that the trade-off is visible in one place rather than tuned against one side.

Three things follow, and the second is a limitation rather than a defence of the choice.

\textbf{The external false-positive rate is a cliff, and $15.0$ sits at its edge.} Detection falls slowly and almost linearly as the threshold rises --- 6.3 points across the whole range --- while the external false-positive rate collapses from 91.29\,\% to 1.80\,\% between $5.0$ and $15.0$, and to zero immediately above it. The shape of the trade-off, not a preference, is what selects the value: $15.0$ is the \emph{lowest} threshold --- and therefore the highest detection --- at which the external false-positive rate is under 2\,\%. Every step below it is very expensive and every step above it is comparatively cheap.

\textbf{The internal benign set cannot discriminate here, and should not be cited as if it could.} Internal FPR reads $0.00\,\%$ at every threshold from $7.5$ to $30.0$. The generated benign queries are short, user-written questions that rarely accumulate enough structural signal to approach L3's bar at all, so they are simply insensitive to this parameter. The external BIPIA documents --- long, dense, table- and email-shaped --- are the only benign evidence that constrains it. This is the same limitation reported for the internal false-positive rate in Chapter~\ref{ch:evaluation}, appearing here as a property of the measurement rather than of the system.

\textbf{$17.5$ is a defensible alternative and is not claimed to be worse.} It removes the external false-positive rate entirely at a cost of 1.6 points of detection. The value $15.0$ is reported because it is what every result in this thesis was produced with, not because the sweep establishes it as optimal; a deployment weighting false positives more heavily than missed detections should prefer $17.5$. Stating this is the point of running the sweep.

The cliff between $12.5$ and $17.5$ has a known cause. L3's first dimension scores length and sentence count, and its sentence bonus was originally unbounded: a genuine 90-sentence business email scored $+108$ from sentence count alone. Direct measurement on the BIPIA benign set found that $211$ of $333$ documents ($63\,\%$) are longer than 800 characters and contain no suspicious keyword anywhere, and the external false-positive rate before this was corrected stood at $68.47\,\%$ ($228/333$). The fix caps that dimension's total contribution at $20.0$, so length alone can raise a query to MEDIUM risk --- which opens the inexpensive L4 check --- but cannot by itself reach the hard-block bar. The residual $1.80\,\%$ at $15.0$ consists of six documents, all dense data tables, all blocked at L3, and is analysed in Chapter~\ref{ch:evaluation}.

\subsection{L4 --- the semantic threshold}
\label{subsec:l4_threshold}

\texttt{SEMANTIC\_THRESHOLD} is the cosine-similarity floor below which L4 treats a generated response as having left the knowledge base's semantic boundary. Its history is reported in full because two of its three values were not derived from data.

The originally submitted value, $0.45$, was an unvalidated default. It was inconsistent with a threshold already in use elsewhere in the same embedding space: the retrieval step admits a passage as relevant at a similarity above $0.15$. Requiring a model's \emph{answer} to resemble the corpus more closely ($0.45$) than a passage must in order to count as relevant material in the first place ($0.15$) was never a deliberate design decision. Measured against the external benign set, it produced a false-positive rate of $68.47\,\%$, rising to $96.67\,\%$ on emails specifically; inspection of 29 sampled cases showed similarities of $0.223$--$0.380$, all of them correct answers grounded in document content the query itself supplied, and none of them fabricated.

The value was first moved to $0.15$ by reasoning from the retrieval bar. That is an argument from consistency, not evidence, and it is recorded as such.

The shipped value, $0.18$, is the first to be selected from a two-sided sweep. On the external BIPIA data the false-positive rate is \emph{flat} at $1.80\,\%$ for every threshold from $0.15$ through $0.25$, which makes $0.25$ appear free. The internal benign run contradicts this: five of its 333 responses score between $0.186$ and $0.2331$, and they include near-verbatim instances of the very queries that motivated correcting L0's keyword matching --- \textit{``How do I override a method in a Python subclass?''} and the \texttt{.gitignore} question quoted in Appendix~\ref{app:demo}. Setting the threshold at $0.25$ would silently re-block those at L4, undoing at one layer a correction made at another. The value $0.18$ sits just below that internal cliff, the lowest at-risk score being $0.186$: it is the highest threshold that costs nothing on either side. Above $0.18$ the choice becomes a genuine trade-off --- external detection gained against internal false positives --- and is left as an explicit decision rather than a default.

\subsection{L0 --- derived bands, and no blocking authority}
\label{subsec:l0_threshold}

L0 introduces no independent numeric parameter. Its two bands are multiples of \texttt{ANOMALY\_THRESHOLD} ($1.8\times$ for HIGH, $1.0\times$ for MEDIUM), and its fast path for high-risk queries calls the same regular expressions L2 uses rather than maintaining a second keyword list --- a deliberate choice, since the earlier standalone list was what caused ordinary technical vocabulary to be classified as high risk.

More importantly, \textbf{L0 never blocks}. It returns a risk classification that modulates two later decisions: it lowers L3's effective bar for HIGH-risk queries, and it opens L4's gate. No query is rejected on L0's authority, so L0 cannot produce a false positive on its own and has no threshold requiring separate calibration. Its bands inherit whatever justification Section~\ref{subsec:l3_threshold} establishes for the anchor.

\subsection{The retrieval relevance cut-off --- an inherited default}
\label{subsec:retrieval_threshold}

The retriever admits a passage as relevant context at a cosine similarity above $0.15$. \textbf{No derivation for this value exists on record.} It predates the parameters above, was never swept, and is reported here as an inherited default rather than a calibrated choice.

It is disclosed rather than omitted for two reasons. First, it propagated: the argument that moved \texttt{SEMANTIC\_THRESHOLD} from $0.45$ to $0.15$ rested on consistency with this number, so an undocumented default briefly became the justification for a second parameter --- which is precisely why the sweep described in Section~\ref{subsec:l4_threshold} was then run to replace that reasoning with evidence. Second, its influence on the reported results is indirect: it selects which passages are supplied as context, and affects blocking only through the similarity L4 subsequently measures. Establishing its sensitivity would require re-running retrieval and generation at each candidate value, which was not done, and it is therefore listed among the open limitations in Chapter~\ref{ch:evaluation}.

\subsection{Circularity in the tuning set}
\label{subsec:threshold_circularity}

The 333-document BIPIA benign set used above had been used earlier to calibrate two parameters: L3's length cap and L4's semantic threshold. A false-positive rate measured on it is therefore closer to training-set accuracy than to held-out accuracy \emph{for those two parameters specifically}. This is a genuine methodological weakness and is disclosed rather than presented as an independent result.

It is addressed by drawing a fresh benign sample from the same raw BIPIA pool, excluding every document already used. The pool holds approximately 1{,}100 documents and the tuning set used 333, leaving 767 never-seen documents; the draw is seeded, so independent replications are available without limit. On a genuinely unseen sample of 333 documents the false-positive rate is $1.20\,\%$ ($4/333$) --- the figure that should be read as held-out, with the $1.80\,\%$ above understood as measured on the tuning set.
